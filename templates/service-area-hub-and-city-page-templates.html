Reusable SEO, AEO, and GEO templates for /service-area/ hub pages and /service-area/{{city-slug}}/ city/location pages.

These templates are based on the provided HTML and JSON-LD standards, including FAQ accordion conventions and LocalBusiness schema structure. Use them as a foundation for scalable, high-quality service-area architecture.

Important: Avoid thin city-swap doorway pages. Each city page should contain unique, locally useful content, not just duplicated copy with the city name changed.

Template 1: /service-area/ Hub Page Layout

SEO Purpose

The hub page should act as the entity bridge between: 

Homepage → Core Service Pages → Service Area Hub → City Pages

Its job is not to rank for every city. Its job is to clearly define:

  • Who the business serves
  • What services are available
  • Which locations are covered
  • How the city pages relate to the main business entity
  • Why the business is qualified across the region

URL

/service-area/

Recommended Meta

<title>{{Primary Service}} Service Areas | {{Business Name}}</title>
<meta name="description" content="{{Business Name}} provides {{primary service}} across {{region/state/metro}}. View our service areas, nearby cities, and local service options.">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://www.example.com/service-area/">

Template 2: City / Location Page Layout

SEO Purpose

Each city page should answer: 

“Does this business provide this service in this specific city, and why should I trust them here?”

The page should be unique because of local proof, not just city-name swaps. Add city-specific details such as neighborhoods, landmarks, response patterns, local regulations, climate/property conditions, project examples, reviews from nearby customers, and city-specific FAQs.

URL Pattern

/service-area/{{city-slug}}/

Example:

/service-area/tacoma-wa/

Recommended Meta

<title>{{Primary Service}} in {{City}}, {{State}} | {{Business Name}}</title>
<meta name="description" content="{{Business Name}} provides {{primary service}} in {{City}}, {{State}} and nearby areas. Call {{Phone Number}} to schedule local service.">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://www.example.com/service-area/{{city-slug}}/">

Recommended Internal Linking Model

Use this structure:

/
├── /services/
│   ├── /services/service-1/
│   ├── /services/service-2/
│   └── /services/service-3/
│
├── /service-area/
│   ├── /service-area/city-1/
│   ├── /service-area/city-2/
│   └── /service-area/city-3/
│
└── /blog/
    ├── local-service-guide-1/
    ├── cost-guide/
    └── seasonal-maintenance-guide/

Each city page should link to:

  • The /service-area/ hub
  • The main service page
  • 2–4 related service pages
  • 2–3 nearby city pages
  • 1–2 relevant local blog/supporting pages
  • Contact or booking page

Each service page should link back to:

  • The service area hub
  • Top-priority city pages
  • Relevant case studies or FAQs

This supports topical authority, entity relationships, and AI answer extraction rather than leaving city pages isolated.

City Page Quality Checklist

  • City page has at least 40–60% unique local content.
  • H1 includes service + city + state.
  • Intro answers “Do you serve this city?” within the first 100 words.
  • Page links to the main service page.
  • Page links back to /service-area/.
  • Page links to nearby relevant city pages.
  • LocalBusiness schema is consistent with the homepage entity.
  • areaServed references the target city.
  • FAQ content matches visible on-page content.
  • No fake address is added for the city.
  • No claim is made that the business has an office in the city unless it truly does.
  • Testimonials, photos, case studies, or examples are genuinely local when used.
  • GBP service area, website service areas, and off-site citations are consistent.

Best-Practice Publishing Rules

For the hub page

Primary intent: regional coverage discovery
Target query pattern: "{{service}} service areas", "{{business}} locations", "{{service}} near {{region}}"
Canonical: self
Index: yes
Schema: LocalBusiness + WebPage + ItemList

For city pages

Primary intent: local service validation
Target query pattern: "{{service}} in {{city}}", "{{service}} near me", "{{business type}} {{city}} {{state}}"
Canonical: self
Index: yes, only when locally useful and unique
Schema: LocalBusiness + Service + WebPage + FAQPage

For low-priority cities with no unique content

Do not publish thin city pages.
Instead, list the city on /service-area/ until you have local proof, customer examples, photos, reviews, or service-specific details.
A strong /service-area/ hub with fewer high-quality city pages is better than 50 near-duplicate location pages.
