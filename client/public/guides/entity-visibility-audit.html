Before AI can recommend you, it has to do its homework.

The Entity Visibility Audit is a one-time check-up: can AI systems actually understand, verify, find, and trust your business enough to recommend it? This page walks through the eight things we score, in plain English.

Also called an AI Search Readiness Audit, a Retrieval Readiness Audit, or an AI Citation & Entity Audit, depending on the client.

Picture it like this

Remember the friend who talks to millions of people a day — the one your AI Visibility report is built around? Before they’ll ever say your name to a stranger, they have to do their homework on you first: figure out who you actually are, confirm other people vouch for you, and make sure they’re not about to send someone to the wrong business by mistake.

An Entity Visibility Audit is a check-up on how well that homework would go, today. It doesn’t track what’s already happening in AI conversations — that’s the report. It’s a one-time readiness score: if an AI assistant sat down right now to research your business from scratch, how far would it get, and how confident would it be?

01
Entity Clarity

Does AI actually know who you are?

Before your friend can vouch for you, they need the basics straight: what the business is actually called, what kind of business it is, what it sells, where it operates, and who’s behind it. If those facts are scattered, contradictory, or missing across the web, AI has to guess — and a guessing AI doesn’t recommend anyone with confidence.

What we check
  • Brand name consistency
  • Organization type
  • Service/product categories
  • Founder, team, and author entities
  • Location and service-area clarity
  • Offerings and differentiators
  • Industry associations
  • External entity references
  • sameAs connections
  • Knowledge graph alignment

Core questionDoes the web clearly explain who this brand is, what it does, who it serves, and why it matters?

Why it matters: this is the foundation everything else sits on — if AI can’t nail down the basic facts, none of the harder-to-earn signals later, like citations or sentiment, can be trusted either.

02
Retrieval Readiness

Can AI actually get in the door and read what’s there?

Even a well-known business can be invisible to AI if the front door is effectively locked — pages that block AI crawlers, broken links, slow-loading pages, or content buried behind scripts the AI can’t run. This checks the plumbing: not whether the story is good, but whether AI can physically reach it.

What we check
  • robots.txt and AI crawler access
  • XML sitemaps
  • Indexation health
  • Page speed and renderability
  • Structured data validity
  • Canonicalization
  • Internal linking
  • Crawl depth
  • JavaScript dependency
  • Server response codes
  • Content freshness
  • Machine-readable tables and sections

Core questionCan AI-powered search systems access, parse, and retrieve the brand’s most important information?

Why it matters: this is the technical layer — no amount of great content matters if the AI literally can’t get to it.

03
Structured Data Coverage

Have you labeled things so a machine can understand them?

A person can read a page and figure out “oh, this is the pricing, this is the address, this is a review.” A machine needs that spelled out in its own language. Structured data — schema markup — is literally labeling the parts of your website so AI doesn’t have to guess what it’s looking at. It’s the difference between a filing cabinet with hand-written folders and one with color-coded, alphabetized labels.

What we check
  • Schema markup presence and validity
  • Coverage across key page types
  • Knowledge graph reinforcement
  • Consistency with on-page facts

Core questionIf a machine skimmed this site instead of a person, would it know what it was looking at?

Why it matters: this is what lets AI lift an exact fact — a price, an address, a service area — straight off your site with confidence, instead of paraphrasing a guess.

04
Prompt Coverage

Does your content answer the questions people actually ask AI?

Nobody types “plumbing services keyword” into ChatGPT. They type “who’s a good emergency plumber near me that won’t rip me off,” or “best plumber vs. handyman for a slow leak.” This checks whether your content is written to answer the real, conversational, sometimes messy questions buyers ask AI — not the tidy keyword phrases search engines used to reward.

What we check
  • High-intent buyer prompts
  • Local recommendation prompts
  • “Best” and “top” prompts
  • “Alternative to” prompts
  • “Versus” prompts
  • Problem-solution prompts
  • Product/service fit prompts
  • Multi-turn decision prompts
  • Objection-handling prompts
  • Post-purchase/support prompts

Core questionWhen buyers ask AI for help, does the brand have content that deserves to appear in the answer?

Why it matters: keyword rankings rewarded matching a phrase. AI rewards actually answering the question — this is the modern equivalent of keyword research.

05
Citation-First Content

Is your content written so AI can quote it directly?

AI doesn’t send people to read your whole page the way Google used to — it lifts a sentence or two and answers with that. If your best information is buried three paragraphs deep in dense marketing copy, AI has nothing clean to grab. This checks whether your key facts sit in short, quotable, extractable blocks an AI can lift word-for-word.

What we check
  • Concise answer blocks on key pages
  • Clear service/product definitions
  • Direct answers to conversational prompts
  • Content extractable without outside context
  • FAQs mapped to real buyer questions
  • Comparison and alternative content
  • Proof, data, examples, expert commentary
  • Structured pricing, features, locations, qualifications

Core questionIs the content built for humans to read and AI systems to quote?

Why it matters: two businesses can say the exact same true thing, and only one of them gets quoted — because it said it in a sentence AI could actually lift.

06
Off-Site Consensus

Do other trusted sources back up your story?

AI doesn’t just take your word for it. It checks whether your business profile, your reviews, your directory listings, and everyone else’s mentions of you all agree — same name, same address, same story. It’s the online version of a background check: if five independent sources describe you the same way, that’s trust an AI can act on.

What we check
  • Google Business Profile
  • Bing Places
  • Apple Business Connect
  • Yelp
  • BBB
  • LinkedIn
  • Crunchbase
  • Wikidata (when relevant)
  • Industry and local directories
  • Review platforms
  • PR and podcast/interview mentions
  • Community/forum references

Core questionDo trusted third-party sources confirm the same facts the brand claims on its own website?

Why it matters: this is corroboration a business can’t fake by editing its own website — it’s other people’s word, and AI weighs that heavily.

07
AI Share of Voice

Right now, does AI actually recommend you over the competition?

This is the baseline snapshot: we run a controlled set of real buyer questions across the major AI platforms and see, plainly, whether you show up, whether you get cited, who shows up instead of you, and what tone AI uses when it does mention you. It’s the starting line for the ongoing tracking your AI Visibility report does every month after.

What we check
  • Whether the brand is mentioned
  • Whether the brand is cited
  • Which competitors appear
  • Which sources are cited
  • Sentiment or positioning attached to each brand
  • Whether the AI answer is accurate
  • Which prompts are immediate opportunities

Core questionHow often does AI recommend this brand compared to competitors?

Why it matters: everything else in this audit explains why this number is what it is — this is the one that turns into next month’s report.

08
Competitor Displacement Opportunity

Why is AI picking them instead of you — and can that change?

For every competitor AI recommends ahead of you, there’s usually a specific, findable reason: a directory listing they have and you don’t, a comparison article shaping the AI’s answer, a review platform it trusts more. This traces each competitor advantage back to its source, so the fix is a specific action, not a vague “be more visible.”

What we check
  • Which competitors AI recommends
  • Which sources AI cites for them
  • Which directories/publications influence that
  • Which comparison pages shape AI’s answers
  • Which review sites act as trust sources
  • Which entity attributes competitors own
  • Where the client can realistically displace them

Core questionWhy is AI choosing competitors, and what source material is causing that recommendation?

Why it matters: this turns “we’re losing to competitor X” into a specific punch list — which sources to target, which pages to build, which listings to claim.

A few likely questions

How is this different from my monthly AI Visibility report?
Think of this audit as the check-up before the treatment plan, and the report as the ongoing chart of your progress. The audit is a deeper, periodic readiness assessment across eight areas — including things the monthly report doesn’t track, like whether AI crawlers can even reach your site, or whether outside directories agree on your business details. This audit’s AI Share of Voice baseline is the starting point the monthly report then tracks going forward.
Do I need to redo this audit regularly?
Not monthly. It’s meant as a periodic reset — typically alongside a major site change, a rebrand, or roughly once or twice a year — to catch drift in the underlying facts and technical access that the ongoing report doesn’t check.
What do I actually get at the end?
  • An Entity Visibility Scorecard (the eight scores plus the overall number)
  • An AI Share of Voice baseline
  • A map of which buyer questions your content does and doesn’t answer
  • Recommendations for labeling your site so machines can read it
  • Quick-win content opportunities
  • A technical access report (can AI even reach the site)
  • Gaps in outside confirmation — reviews, directories, mentions
  • A breakdown of why competitors get picked over you
  • A 90-day roadmap to close the gaps
